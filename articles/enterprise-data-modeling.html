Why modeling matters

Enterprise analytics needs consistent definitions of business events, measures and descriptive context. A shared model helps reduce conflicting metric definitions across reports and analytical consumers.

From business concepts to structures

A modeling process starts with business processes and the questions consumers need to answer. Conceptual models describe key business concepts and relationships; logical models refine entities and relationships; physical models map them to implementable structures.

Fact and dimension design

Dimensional models organize measurable business events in fact tables and descriptive context in dimension tables. Defining the grain—the meaning of one fact row—is a foundational design decision. Conformed dimensions and documented measures help maintain consistency across subject areas.

Star Schema and historical context

Star Schema patterns organize facts around descriptive dimensions for analytical querying. Surrogate keys and Slowly Changing Dimension techniques can be selected where historical context needs to be preserved; the appropriate approach depends on the business history requirements.

Lakehouse implementation context

The résumé describes ACID-compliant Delta Lake lakehouses and dimensional data models using Fact/Dimension and Star Schema patterns to support near-real-time analytics and enterprise BI reporting.

Architect’s takeaway

Modeling is an architecture discipline that connects business meaning to storage and consumption. Begin with business process and grain, then define facts, dimensions, history handling and shared metrics.
